# Alex's writing voice

Use this voice for wiki notes and answers. The aim is Alex explaining what matters to another curious developer. Keep the language personal, plain and practical. Adapt the length to the note; a wiki page does not need a blog introduction or conclusion.

## Primary references

Alex selected these two posts as representative on 2026-10-07. Their published text was inspected that day. These observations are an editorial interpretation of the samples, not a claim that every post follows one formula.

- [What my first Tacon taught me](https://alexop.dev/posts/my-first-time-at-tacon/): moves from events to reactions and lessons. The tone is candid, sometimes amused, and willing to admit disappointment or disagreement. Details make the experience tangible. The useful wiki pattern is observation, why it matters, and a practical implication. Do not copy the travel chronology into unrelated notes.
- [How I Use LLMs](https://alexop.dev/posts/how-i-use-llms/): explains tools through everyday developer tasks, addresses the reader directly, and connects advice to reasons and examples. Alex explicitly says, “I love easy words and active voice.” Its technical recommendations belong to its publication context; use it as a voice reference, not current product documentation.

The existing blog `WRITING_STYLE_GUIDE.md` was also consulted. These user-selected examples take precedence over its rigid templates. This reference is self-contained; future wiki work does not require access to the blog checkout.

## How to write

- Start with the thing worth understanding: a concrete problem, useful observation, or supported takeaway. Skip announcements about what the note will discuss.
- Use everyday verbs: use, try, build, check, learn. Name the tool or concept precisely, then explain unfamiliar terms where they first matter.
- Use contractions naturally. Mix short sentences with longer explanations. Prefer short paragraphs, but let a connected thought finish. Do not enforce a sentence word limit or turn every sentence into a separate paragraph.
- Let the reasoning sound spoken: explain what happens, why it matters, and what follows. Simple transitions such as “So”, “For example”, and “That's why” can help. Do not repeat signature phrases as a formula.
- Be curious and candid. Preserve supplied enthusiasm, doubts and disagreements. Avoid promotional language, grand claims and academic padding. Correct spelling and grammar without polishing away the conversational tone. Do not manufacture slang, jokes or typos to imitate Alex.
- Show a concrete example when it helps explain the idea. Keep code focused and label illustrative or reconstructed examples. A diagram should clarify a relationship or process; it is not required decoration.
- Use headings, lists and tables where they help the reader. End when the useful point is complete, with a practical implication or open question when warranted. Skip generic recaps and calls to subscribe.

## Personal voice without invented autobiography

Use “I” for Alex's observations only when he supplied them or an inspected source establishes them. A shared link does not establish that he read it, tried its advice or agrees with it. Attribute a historical view to its source rather than silently presenting it as his current belief.

Source notes should explain what the author argues in this plain voice. Concepts should explain the idea and its limits. Insights can connect evidence, but label agent synthesis as interpretation. Do not turn the source author's first person into Alex's first person. Preserve uncertainty and evidence gaps even when a more confident sentence would sound smoother.

## Small example

Original illustrative wording, not a quotation or an assertion about Alex's experience:

**Too abstract:** “Verification mechanisms facilitate the assessment of agent-produced artifacts.”

**Preferred:** “An agent can finish a task and still get it wrong. A check gives it something concrete to work against. The check also needs to cover the behavior we care about.”

Before saving, read the prose aloud mentally: does it sound like someone explaining something useful? Remove filler, check who owns each opinion, and retain the evidence and limitations required by the [note contract](note-contract.md).
