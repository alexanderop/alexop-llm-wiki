## The advice

> Talk to Claude the way you would a coworker.

Cherny argues that, for most tasks, specifying a goal is more useful than prescribing every step. He contrasts this with his experience of the Sonnet 3.5 era, when he says prompt formulation mattered more. His three priorities are the desired outcome, the amount of effort to spend, and the way the model should check its result.

This is Cherny's practitioner advice about Claude, not a measured comparison across models or a claim that detailed constraints never matter.

## Why this is useful

Our interpretation is that a prompt can function as a delegation brief: state the result, set an effort expectation, and make success observable. This is the organizing idea behind [Goal-oriented prompting](/notes/goal-oriented-prompting).

The verification element connects directly to [Agent verification loops](/notes/agent-verification-loops): a requested check becomes useful when the agent can execute the relevant behavior, inspect evidence, and revise its work. The post names the responsibility; the concept describes how to close the loop.

Cherny's [Home Depot artifact prompt](/notes/boris-cherny-home-depot-prompt) provides a related example of outcome and effort language. Its visible text does not specify an equally concrete verification procedure, a useful limit when comparing the two sources.
