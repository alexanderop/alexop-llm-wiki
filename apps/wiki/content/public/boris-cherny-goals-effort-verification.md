Boris Cherny's prompting advice starts with a simple comparison:

> Talk to Claude the way you would a coworker.

For most tasks, he recommends giving Claude a goal and letting it work out the steps. He says prompt wording mattered more in the Sonnet 3.5 era. His current advice in this post focuses on three things: what you want, how much effort the task deserves, and how Claude should verify the result.

This is advice from his experience with Claude. The post doesn't offer a measured comparison across models or show that detailed constraints never matter.

## Why this is useful

One way to apply the advice is to treat the prompt like a brief for a coworker. Explain the result you're looking for, how much work to put into it, and what would show that it's done. That's the interpretation developed in [Goal-oriented prompting](/notes/goal-oriented-prompting).

The verification part needs more than a sentence in a prompt. The agent needs to run the relevant behavior, inspect what happened, and act on the result. [Agent verification loops](/notes/agent-verification-loops) explains that process.

Cherny's [Home Depot prompt](/notes/boris-cherny-home-depot-prompt) gives a concrete example of describing an outcome and asking for more effort. Its visible text is less clear about how to verify the finished work. That makes it useful to compare with this advice.
